Chapter Twenty-Two

An Offered Way Back

Laura was at the dining table when he came in. She had the venue plans open across it, the corners held down with the tape measure and two of the terracotta pots, and she did not get up. She looked at him for a while before she said anything.

He put his keys in the bowl by the door. He had nothing else to put down.

“Come and sit,” she said.

He sat across from her. The plans were the garden with the cedars, drawn to scale, the chairs in their rows. She had made a cross in pencil where the bride’s mother would sit.

“Tell me what Graham said. Not what you told me on the phone. What he said.”

He told her. Your employment ends today. I don’t trust you with our systems after this. She listened with the pencil in her hand, and when he finished she set it down on the drawing hard enough to break the point, and looked at the broken point and not at him.

“And Aaron?”

“He’s restoring his program. He’ll have the test running again by now.”

“So you cost him a morning.” Her voice was level. He knew that voice. It was the one she used with a contractor who had done the work wrong, and she had never used it on him. “A morning, and your job, and whatever you were going to be to those people when they needed you.” She pressed her hands flat on the plans, and he saw that they were not steady. “Somebody has been sitting in a car watching Sarah because of this man, and you went in this morning and gave him a reason.”

He had not put the two things beside each other until she did. He sat with it and could not answer her.

“I was going to say I’m sorry,” she said. “I’m not sure I am. You did a thing you knew they would fire you for, and now they have, and I don’t know what you expected to feel.”

“Relief.”

“Did it come?”

“No.”

She reached across the plans and took his hand. Her grip hurt. She held on until he stopped trying to take his hand back, and for a while after that, and when she let go the marks of her fingers stayed on his.

“Janet called. She’s bringing lunch.” Laura looked at the drawings between them. “I’m leaving these where they are. I have to be at the venue at two, and I’m going, and you are not going to call anyone to say I can’t.”

❦

Janet arrived with three sandwiches and an apology for the pickle that had leaked through the bag. Laura took the bag from her before she could set it on the dining table. The noon light came through the window onto the plans. He could not remember the last time he had sat at this table in the middle of a weekday. Nothing on it was his.

“I’ll get plates,” Laura said. “Can somebody move those plans?”

Sam stood, but Janet had already gathered them up. He took the plates Laura handed him and set one at each place.

“I thought you had afternoon appointments,” he said to Janet.

“Priya is seeing them. Aaron’s finished the test with Alderwick.”

Already. Sam put the last plate down too hard.

“He restored the program from his backup,” Janet went on. “They ran the test again, and it worked.”

“How many appointments did Priya have to reschedule?”

“Three. Two can come back this afternoon.”

Laura pulled out a chair. Her hand rested briefly against Sam’s back as she passed.

He sat. The sandwich was turkey, with the tomato wrapped separately. Laura opened the smaller packet and divided the slices with her fingers.

“Evelyn called me,” Janet said. “She’ll speak to you at twelve thirty.”

“Is Dambrosky joining?”

“No,” Janet said. “I asked for a conversation without him or Delmere’s staff.”

“Have you told Evelyn what happened to James?”

“I told her Dambrosky removed him from the project.” Janet unwrapped her sandwich. “I thought you should tell her how you found out.”

Sam looked at Laura. She met his eyes and waited.

“James showed me Dambrosky’s assessment of me,” he said. “He told me what Dambrosky had asked him about my family. He wanted to tell our lawyer himself. I promised I wouldn’t identify him.”

Janet left off folding the wrapper.

“I told Dambrosky that James had given me the assessment.” Sam looked down at his untouched sandwich. “I thought he’d leave my family alone if he knew James was going to speak to our lawyer. Dambrosky picked up the phone while I was still sitting there and said they needed another engineer.”

“Have you spoken to James since?” Janet asked.

“He asked if I’d told Dambrosky. I said yes. I haven’t heard from him since.”

“Then give him some time.” Janet put down the wrapper. “The directors agreed this morning: nothing goes to Delmere until they’ve heard from the people watching at Alderwick. Our lawyers asked Evelyn to make sure of it. I asked her to invite you to the examination.”

Laura pushed his plate an inch closer. Sam took a bite and chewed until he could swallow it. For several minutes Laura asked Janet about the new counselors. One had reorganized the supply cupboard before lunch on her first day.

Sam listened. The job went on without him. People had arrived yesterday who would know him mainly as the man dismissed on their second day.

When Janet’s phone rang, Laura stood to collect the wrappers. She squeezed Sam’s shoulder, then carried the plates into the kitchen.

❦

Evelyn wore the green cardigan Sam recognized from their meetings. Behind her, a man passed carrying a stepladder. When he had gone, she told Sam somebody was finally replacing the smoke alarm outside her office.

Janet placed her tablet between herself and Sam. Laura stayed in the kitchen, rinsing the plates. Sam could hear the water running behind him.

“I asked to speak before you appointed Dambrosky,” Sam said. “You knew he’d paid someone to watch Laura. Why did you want him on the board?”

“He’s offered to raise money as a director, not just give us a list of names,” Evelyn answered. “We’d spend years building the contacts he can bring us now.” She pulled the edge of her cardigan over her wrist. “I thought we could make him stop watching your family and still accept his help.”

“When Laura asked him to stop, he wouldn’t say he would,” Sam reminded her.

“I know. I read Graham’s message before we voted.”

Evelyn’s gaze shifted away from the camera, then returned. She had pushed her reading glasses up into her hair, and her voice kept the level it had in board meetings. “He can’t vote on the purchase, Sam. We can. Tell me what you’re asking us to do.”

“Reject it. Don’t sell Delmere the right to do whatever they want with it afterward.”

“I want to be able to tell Janet we can hire more counselors,” Evelyn said. “I’m not ready to turn the offer down.”

Sam kept his hands below the edge of the table. “Dambrosky knows the Guard took everyone out of three towns at gunpoint and burned what they left behind.”

“We’re being asked to sell the model,” Evelyn said. “We won’t be choosing their recruits. What goes out under our name is ours to answer for. What Delmere owns is Delmere’s. I’m not going to pretend we can follow the model everywhere it goes.”

Sam leaned closer to the tablet. “He’ll offer a veteran work that feels as if it was made for him. My model will have found it in his record. ADICUS will tell the recruiter what the man needs to hear to say yes. By the time the man learns why they hired him, he’s got the same bills and the same family depending on him. And his name is on what he moved, not theirs.”

“What am I supposed to tell the next man who calls me afterward?” Sam pressed. “He’s home by then. Whatever they asked him to do, he’s already done it. I won’t help you sell this, Evelyn.”

Sam put a hand on the table, saw his fingers trembling and pressed his palm flat against the wood. Janet looked down at his hand, then back at his face.

“Do you need a minute?” Evelyn asked.

“No. I want you to say you won’t sell it.”

Laura brought him a glass of water. She set it beside his hand and rested her own on his shoulder. Sam kept his eyes on the screen.

Evelyn moved closer to her camera. “I don’t want anyone recruited for what you’re describing,” she said. “We haven’t agreed to the sale.”

“Will you ask the others to reject it?”

“I’ll tell the other directors why you want us to turn it down,” Evelyn replied. “I haven’t decided how I’ll vote.”

Sam looked to Janet. She was sitting straight, her hands loose on her lap. She had not interrupted him.

“Janet asked us to appoint you to examine the installation,” Evelyn went on. “The directors agreed. Graham knows. You would attend with Janet and an Alderwick employee, using the equipment they provide. You would see how Delmere’s recruiters intend to use the model and explain how its recommendations would help them select people.”

“After Graham fired me.”

“Yes,” Evelyn said. “Graham told us what you did. He also said you could check how Delmere is using the recommendations. We need to know if they’re offering someone a job without the training your model says that person still needs.”

Sam could hear water dripping into the kitchen sink. Laura had turned off the tap.

“Could I examine the files before Friday?”

“Yes. During an arranged session with Alderwick,” Evelyn said. “You don’t have to recommend delivery. Tell us what you find, including anything they refuse to show you.”

Sam moved back from the tablet. He imagined standing behind Sandra, the Alderwick employee, asking her to open a file and looking on while she found it. He would have to explain each comparison he wanted her to run. Aaron would be there, watching him ask for things he could have done in seconds at his own desk.

“I want my old access restored,” he said.

“You can’t have that,” Evelyn said.

“Then you aren’t asking me to do the job properly.”

Janet faced him. “It’s at Alderwick, Friday morning. I can drive you, if you want company.”

“Email me the details,” he said.

“I will,” Evelyn said. “Please answer today.”

When the call ended, Janet closed the tablet cover. She did not begin another argument.

“Thank you for coming,” Sam said.

“Somebody had to bring lunch.” Janet stood.

Sam helped her find her coat. At the door, she asked Laura for an apple. Laura put two in her hand.

“For Priya,” Laura said.

Janet said she would make sure Priya got one. Laura went back to the dining table, and Sam walked Janet to the car.

While she put the apples on the passenger seat, he checked his phone. Nothing from Nadia.

“Our lawyers are speaking to Leila,” Janet said. “Nadia wants the directors to hear her before they vote.”

“Nadia and Amir still have their phones?”

“Nadia replied before I came here. She said their manager, Vale, was keeping them outside his office. Leila was trying to get there.”

❦

Jasleen called while Laura was changing for the site visit. Sam answered in the kitchen, by the three washed plates drying on the rack. Jasleen’s voice was lower than it had been in the training room. Behind it he heard a door close and people pass, an office going on with its day.

“Dambrosky has told James’s replacement to connect your model to ADICUS for Friday, and to give Mission Support’s recruiters the use of it too,” Jasleen said. “Victor forwarded the instruction to me. He thought James had left that part unfinished.”

“You told him why James stopped it?”

“Yes. Martin called him after we spoke and asked about giving the job to someone who could finish it by Friday. Victor is going ahead.”

“And you’re letting them?”

“I asked our chief executive to keep James on the project. I was told Martin could replace him.” She stopped, then went on. “I’ve objected to including Mission Support. I haven’t persuaded anyone above him to stop it.”

“You sent us the offer to buy the model.”

“I did. I wanted it for our recruiters. I still think they could use it well.” She paused. “I thought James could keep Mission Support from using the model. You told me Martin intended to use it anyway. I was wrong about him.”

Sam closed his eyes. Nadia and Amir were outside Vale’s office. Sam imagined Leila arriving to find Nadia and Amir gone, with no one willing to tell her where they had been taken.

“Have you heard from Leila?”

“She couldn’t get in. Vale says the Guard wants to question Nadia and Amir, and he is arranging transport. She has asked him to let them leave with her instead. I’m asking too.”

“Can you order Vale to let them leave?”

“He doesn’t report to me. Celia Trent can tell him. She’s our chief operating officer. I’ve asked her to call him now.”

“And has she?”

“She hasn’t called me back.”

Sam looked through the kitchen window. A bird landed on the rim of one of Laura’s pots, pecked once at the soil and flew away.

“Evelyn has asked me to come to Alderwick,” he said.

“I know. I asked Janet to speak to her.”

Sam rested his forehead against the cupboard. The wood was cool.

“Will you be there?”

“Yes. I want the directors to see the instruction Dambrosky gave Victor. I’ll send them the message Victor forwarded, and I’ll tell them why I objected.”

“You could refuse to work on the installation.”

“I could. Then Martin could send someone else on Friday.” Jasleen was quiet. “I brought you that offer, Sam. I’m coming. Let him explain to the directors why he overruled me.”

He lifted his head from the cupboard and gripped the edge of the counter. He had gripped a cot this way once, in a tent, while his RP told a Marine outside that the chaplain was unavailable and gave him a time. He had not got up.

“Sam?”

“I’m here.”

“Please answer Janet. Leila and I will keep trying to get Nadia and Amir out of that office.”

He said he would read Janet’s email when it arrived. After the call he put the phone on the dining table and sat.

Laura came downstairs carrying her boots. She placed them on a sheet of newspaper by the front door and went back for the rolled site plans.

“What did Jasleen say?” she asked when she returned.

Sam told her about James’s replacement and the two employees. He did not name them or repeat what they had told him.

“Are you going Friday?”

“I don’t know.”

Laura put the plans down and sat beside him. She looked at his hands.

“You’re still shaking.”

“I’m tired. I’ll be all right.”

“You’re barely sleeping. You hardly touched your lunch.” She placed her hand beside his on the table. “I’m not leaving you in this house like this.”

“You have to go to the venue.”

“I can stay while you answer Janet. Or while you call James, if he’ll speak to you.” She waited, but he said nothing. “I’m glad you called me this morning. I wish you’d called before you changed Aaron’s program.”

“You’d have told me not to.”

“I would have asked you to come home. We could have called Janet together.”

He looked at the phone. Janet had come anyway, with lunch and an offer he hadn’t answered. He could not face calling her to say he wanted more time.

“Would you talk to Don?” Laura asked. “You used to be on the phone with him for hours.”

Don was the one who called and sent photographs. Sam couldn’t remember if he had gone back to answer the invitation. When he thought of Don he still saw the boy from South Carolina, who could sit beside him for an hour without a word and did not mind if Sam did the same.

“It’s been a long time,” he said.

“He asked us down when Sarah left for college. We never picked a weekend.”

“And now I call to tell him I’ve been fired.”

“I’d want Ellen to call me if something like this happened to her.” Laura left her hand beside his. “I think he’d want to hear from you.”

“Do you want me to go down there?”

“I meant call him. We can talk about a visit when I get home. I’d like to see them too.”

He reached for her hand and held it. She stayed for several minutes. Her palm was rough from the week’s work. She turned her hand once so that her fingers closed over his. He could hear the refrigerator in the kitchen, and a car went by that he did not get up to watch.
