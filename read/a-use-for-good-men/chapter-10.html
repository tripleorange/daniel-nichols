Chapter Ten

The First Refusal

Sam held Graham’s office door while Janet carried a chair into the corridor. Len sat down to make a call. He balanced the card she handed him on his knee and asked if he should say Civic Passage was paying.

“Tell them you’re the client,” Janet told him. “Graham has agreed to cover the advice. If you’d rather speak to a different firm, we can help you find one.”

Len turned the card over. “This one’s fine to start with.”

He called the number, then looked up at Sam and waited. Sam took his hand off the door.

“I’ll be in here,” he said.

“I know where you’re going.” Len pointed toward the office with the card. “Go.”

Janet followed Sam inside and closed the door. Graham was behind his desk, putting his glasses on. He had taken his jacket off and hung it on the back of the door. The desk had nothing on it but the keyboard, the monitor and a pad turned to a clean page. The report about Meredan was open on his monitor. Sam could see the photograph of a roof with one corner folded into a room.

“You’ve read it,” Sam said.

“I have.” Graham lowered himself into his chair. “I’ve asked Jasleen to find out who approved Len’s assignment and why Rusk is giving us a different account. I’ve also asked our outside counsel to examine the work Delmere is doing for this force.”

Sam remained standing beside the visitor chair. “I want you to stop the delivery. The model stays at Alderwick. Delmere doesn’t get it.”

Janet sat down. Graham looked from her to Sam, then indicated the chair. Sam sat with his bag between his feet.

“We won’t send anyone else to work for the Meredan Regional Guard while that examination is under way,” Graham said. “Janet is telling the counselors. I’ve told Jasleen the same thing.”

“Len didn’t apply to work for the Guard. He applied for a transport job,” Sam corrected.

“Then we’ll ask who receives the shipment before we consider another referral,” Janet put in. “I’ve already stopped two requests until the clients answer that question.”

Sam looked at her. He hadn’t known there were two requests.

“Thank you,” he said. Then he faced Graham. “That won’t stop Delmere using the model to find people themselves.”

“They can’t have it until the board votes to send it. You know that.”

“I’m asking you to tell the board we shouldn’t give it to them.”

“And I’m telling you I want the examination before I make that recommendation.” Graham restlessly tapped a finger against his leg. “I am not canceling a three-year partnership this afternoon.”

Sam looked at the damaged house on the screen. In the story below it, a man described returning for his mother’s medication and being turned away at the road. There was nothing difficult about understanding it.

“Rusk told Len it was coastal relief,” Sam said. “There was ammunition in with the relief stores. He found out who it was for from a message, after the trucks had gone.”

“The referral says transport,” Graham said. “That’s what the board will ask me about. I don’t have an answer for them yet.”

“Delmere can stop supplying the Guard. Why are we helping them send the next shipment?”

“What if the lawyer tells us the contract is legal?”

“I’d still want us to stop helping Delmere supply that force,” Sam insisted.

Graham took off his glasses and set them down by the keyboard. “I want to stop helping supply the Guard, Sam. I need to know what we’ve agreed to provide and if we can stop that part of the work. I won’t ask the board to cancel the whole agreement until I have those answers.”

Sam’s hands tightened on the strap of his bag.

“Dambrosky runs the people who arranged the work,” Sam said. “He told me the advisers could leave and be paid. Now Rusk is changing his story about when Len learned who the shipment was for. Let him come and explain that to us.”

“Ask James to arrange it,” Graham said.

“Tomorrow,” Sam pressed. “At Alderwick. Before anyone does any more work joining our model to their system.”

Graham glanced at Janet. She was looking at Sam.

“Dambrosky should hear the questions himself,” she said. “James can’t answer for him.”

“All right,” Graham conceded. “I’ll ask Jasleen to make him available.”

Sam loosened his grip on the strap. He could hear Len through the closed door, too faintly to distinguish words. For several seconds he listened instead of asking the next question.

“The first payment arrived this morning,” Graham said.

“I know what day it is,” Sam said.

“I know you do. The two counselors Janet hired start next week. They have both given notice at their old jobs. If we end the agreement, Delmere stops paying. Those two salaries come out of that money, and the advances go back. We have to decide what we can still afford.”

Janet turned her phone facedown on her knee. Sam wanted her to say those two jobs were no reason to give Delmere the model, and knew what it would cost her to say it.

“Are you asking me to keep working because you’ve already spent the money?”

“Ruth’s client wants her back to help their managers prepare for a power failure,” Graham said. “I want her to be able to do that work. I want those two counselors here next week, seeing veterans who’ve been waiting for an appointment.”

Sam bent to lift his bag, then let it rest again. He could leave. Graham could send Priya and Aaron to Alderwick without him. Sam had not even told them he wanted the board to withhold the model.

“I’ll go to Alderwick,” he said. “I want Dambrosky there, and I want the board to hear what he tells us.”

“Send Janet and me an account of what he says,” Graham said. “We’ll both speak to the directors before they decide about the model.”

Janet nodded. “I’ll be available tomorrow. Call me.”

Sam stood and picked up his bag.

Outside, Len was putting the card into his wallet. His Civic Passage counselor was at the copier, searching her phone for the lawyer’s address.

“I’m seeing the lawyer this afternoon,” Len told Sam. “She wants to hear from me before anybody contacts Rusk again.”

“I won’t call him,” Sam promised. “Can James and the people at Alderwick look at the referral and your messages with me?”

“Yes. The people who arranged that job can see the messages too. I want them to explain why Rusk is saying I knew.”

Len took up his bag. “I’ve got to call Denise before she starts looking at flights.”

“Are you going home?”

“After I speak to the lawyer. I’ll decide then.”

At the corridor corner, Len stopped to answer his phone. Sam heard him say that yes, he had eaten something. There was irritation in his voice, then a short laugh.

Sam went down to his car. Before starting it, he sent James the messages and the article, asked him to get Dambrosky to Alderwick, and opened the team’s notes.

Priya and Aaron had finished their checks. Near the end, Priya had asked for time to show James something she had improved. Sam read the question twice and left the cursor there.

He drove north with the sun on his left and the traffic thick at every exit until Gaithersburg, where it thinned. The trees along the highway had begun to turn at the tops. He noticed them, and noticed that he was counting the exits to Frederick, which he had not done in years. Twice he tried out the sentence he would say to Priya, and each time he heard Graham’s words in it. Where the highway crossed the river below Frederick, the water was low and brown. He was across it before he had finished looking.

James called while Sam was on the road. Dambrosky would be at Alderwick in the morning.

❦

Laura was taking leaves off a stem of rosemary when Sam came into the kitchen. She had come in from a site. There was soil on one knee of her jeans, her forearms were wet from the sink, and the rosemary was from the pot by the back step. Two potatoes sat on a cutting board. There was a third in the sink, where it appeared to have escaped washing.

“Catch that one,” she said.

He washed it, dried it and brought it over. She passed him the knife.

“Small enough to cook before we’re too old to eat.”

He cut the three potatoes in half, then into quarters. Laura looked at the pieces.

“Smaller.”

He cut them again. She tipped the pieces into a bowl and poured oil over them. He waited until she put the bottle down.

“Len came to the office today.”

“From Charleston?”

“He flew up yesterday. There’s trouble with the new assignment. Graham is paying for him to see a lawyer.”

Laura moved the bowl aside. “Is he safe?”

“He’s at a hotel. He hasn’t told me anyone is trying to hurt him.” Sam put down the knife. “I can’t tell you his private business. But there are soldiers in Meredan forcing people out of their towns and keeping them out. It’s in the news. Delmere may be supplying the soldiers doing it.”

“Through the work you’re helping them with?”

“They recruited Len through us. I asked Graham to stop delivery of the model. He won’t do that yet. Tomorrow I’m meeting the man who runs the part of Delmere arranging these jobs.”

“Dambrosky?”

“Yes.”

Laura touched the edge of the bowl and left it where it was. “The man you couldn’t stop thinking about after you met him.”

Sam nodded.

“What are you hoping he’ll tell you?”

“That they aren’t doing it.” Sam rubbed one palm over his eyes. “But I don’t think he’s going to. I think he’s going to tell me why I should agree to help.”

Laura put the potatoes on a tray. Sam watched her spread them apart with the spoon, making room for each piece to brown.

“I used to wonder what happened after a man left me,” he said. “Whether he went back to the same work. Whether there was anybody else he could tell.”

Laura turned toward him.

“Now I help people find work. I can show you what we offered them and if they got paid. I thought I could at least be certain about what I was helping them do. I don’t know how to help Delmere use my model after reading that report.”

She opened the oven and put the tray inside. Heat passed against his legs. When she stood, she stayed close enough that he had to face her.

“I’m glad you told me,” she said. “Stay down here tonight.”

“I should speak to the two people coming with me tomorrow.”

“Have you told them what happened?”

“No.”

“What do they think they’re coming to do?”

“Help James join our model to ADICUS. Priya and Aaron have finished checking that our results come through unchanged, and they want to go through them with him.”

Laura’s eyes went to his bag by the door. “Then they should hear from you before they get there.”

He picked up the knife, looked at the empty board, and set it down again.

“They’ve spent years on this with me,” he said. “Priya wants to show him something she’s proud of. I was pleased about it on Friday. I don’t want to be the person who tells her we may have to stop.”

“Neither would I,” Laura agreed. “But I’d rather hear it from you tonight than watch you argue about it while I tried to work out why you’d brought me.”

“I’ll call them,” he said.

“How long?”

“Twenty minutes. I’ll tell you if we need more.”

She set the timer for the potatoes. “Then there should be dinner when you finish.”

He called Priya from the living room and added Aaron. He told them, in the order it had happened, about the roof folded into a room, about asking Graham to stop the delivery, and about Graham saying no. Dambrosky had agreed to come to Alderwick and answer for it. Then he broke off and let them ask. Aaron spoke first, as he did in every meeting, and Sam could hear him eating something while he listened. Priya said nothing for a while, the way she went quiet over a result before she said it was wrong.

Priya asked whether Sam wanted them there.

“I do,” he said. “You know what we’ve built. I want you to hear what they’re asking us to help them do, and I want to hear what you think.”

“Then send us the article,” Aaron said. “We can read it before tomorrow.”

Sam sent the link while they were on the phone. He answered their questions until Laura came to the doorway, holding two plates. Twenty minutes had passed.

He told them he was going to eat with his wife. Priya said she would see him in the morning.

Laura put the plates on the table and took the potatoes from the oven. Sam left his phone on the counter and brought the glasses. When they sat down, she offered to tell him what the florist had done with her order.
