A NEW MANUSCRIPT

A Use for Good Men

In 2003, Sam Artell was a Navy chaplain with the Marines in Iraq, the man people found when they needed someone to hold what they couldn’t say. Nineteen years later he builds software for a veterans’ nonprofit. Where a résumé says “warehouse support,” his model reads the service record and sees an emergency logistics coordinator.

A defense contractor will pay twelve million dollars to license it, enough to hire counselors for veterans stuck on a waiting list. Then one of the Marines Sam counseled in Iraq takes a job through the deal and learns, after the trucks have gone, that he helped ship mortar rounds to a militia driving families from their homes.

The executive behind that shipment, Martin Dambrosky, was waiting at the end of the worst road of Sam’s war. When the company starts photographing Sam’s wife and daughter, his lifelong need to be the one who stops things begins to hurt the people he most wants to protect.

Now the model is due to go to the contractor on Friday, and two whistleblowers are being held at the company’s compound overseas. This time Sam can’t be the one who stops it. He needs help from colleagues he has deceived.

Dambrosky knows how badly Sam needs to save someone. He has always had a use for good men.
